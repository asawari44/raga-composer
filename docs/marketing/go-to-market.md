# Go-to-market plan for Swaramala

Swaramala is a free, open source web app for composing and practicing Hindustani classical music. You can use it in the browser at `https://asawari44.github.io/raga-composer/`. The URL can change later if the repository is renamed. The site has no accounts and no server. Learners can practice with raga reference data, swara notation, generated phrases, tanpura, tabla, piano, harmonium voices, local saved lines, and browser-based recording.

## Positioning

### Positioning statement

For Hindustani classical learners, teachers, and musicians who want a practical way to explore raga notation and practice ideas, Swaramala is a free browser-based composition and practice app that combines raga rules, swara notation, tanpura, tabla, and instrument playback without accounts or paid access.

### Tagline

Compose and practice raga phrases in your browser.

### Tagline alternatives

- A free raga practice desk for learners and teachers.
- Explore swaras, taals, and bandish notation in one browser tab.
- Build raga-true phrases with tanpura, tabla, and notation.

## Audiences and segments

### Students

Students need repeatable practice, notation support, and clear raga facts between lessons. They may search for specific ragas, bandish notation, or online tanpura and tabla tools.

### Teachers and gurus

Teachers need a no-cost tool they can share with students for reference, demonstrations, and homework. They care about accuracy, respectful source handling, and the ability to use the app without accounts.

### Music schools

Music schools need tools that support group learning and practice outside class. They may share Swaramala in class groups, course notes, newsletters, and student portals.

### Hobbyists

Hobbyists want a welcoming path into Hindustani classical music. They may not know how to read notation yet, so clear onboarding, videos, and examples matter.

### Diaspora learners

Diaspora learners often study remotely and need flexible, self-guided practice. They may search in English and share tools through family, community, temple, cultural, and school networks.

### Instrumentalists

Instrumentalists need fast raga reference, scale playback, and phrase ideas that they can adapt to piano, harmonium, violin, flute, guitar, or other instruments.

## Six-week launch plan

### Week 1: Prepare the launch base

- Publish the static site at `https://asawari44.github.io/raga-composer/`.
- Add metadata, Open Graph tags, a social image, `sitemap.xml`, and a web manifest.
- Record 3 short screen captures:
  - Search for raga Yaman and view aaroh, avroh, and pakad.
  - Turn on tanpura and tabla, choose a taal, and play a phrase.
  - Open a composition, play it, and move it into Compose.
- Create a short issue template for teachers to report raga data corrections.

### Week 2: Launch through warm networks

- Send the WhatsApp teacher message to 10 to 20 teachers, students, and school contacts.
- Ask each contact for one of these actions:
  - Try the app for one raga they teach.
  - Share it with one student group.
  - Report one correction or missing raga priority.
- Post the first short demo on Instagram and YouTube Shorts.
- Add a pinned GitHub issue that lists known data limitations and invites review.

### Week 3: Publish community posts

- Post to Reddit only after checking each community's self-promotion rules.
- Use the Reddit drafts that ask for feedback rather than promotion.
- Reply to every substantive comment with specific follow-up.
- Track recurring requests, such as raga coverage, mobile layout, notation conventions, or teacher handouts.

### Week 4: Reach schools and newsletters

- Contact music schools, Gandharva Mahavidyalaya-related communities, and online lesson platforms.
- Offer a short teacher note that explains:
  - The app is free and open source.
  - It runs in the browser with no account.
  - Students should treat it as practice support, not a replacement for a teacher.
- Ask 3 teachers to test one classroom activity.

### Week 5: Launch to maker communities

- Post the Show HN draft.
- Launch on Product Hunt if the site, social image, and analytics are working.
- Share a technical note that explains the static architecture, Web Audio use, and open source data review needs.
- Invite contributions for localization and raga data corrections.

### Week 6: Review, improve, and repeat

- Review analytics, GitHub issues, and direct feedback.
- Fix the top onboarding blockers.
- Publish 2 more short demos based on real questions.
- Decide whether to build the next growth asset:
  - Per-raga landing pages.
  - A teacher handout.
  - Hindi, Marathi, or Bengali landing-page localization.

## SEO plan

### Immediate work

- Use the primary title `Swaramala: compose and practice Hindustani classical music`.
- Use a concise description that includes raga phrases, notation, tanpura, tabla, and free browser app.
- Add `sitemap.xml`. A project site can't serve `robots.txt`, because crawlers only read it from the domain root; submit the sitemap in Google Search Console instead.
- Add JSON-LD for a free `WebApplication` in the `Music` category.
- Make the GitHub repository description match the site description.
- Use descriptive headings on the page for raga reference, compositions, alaap and taan, compose, tanpura, tabla, and keyboard.

## Localization priorities

### Hindi

Prioritize Hindi because many learners in India search and share music-learning resources in Hindi. Start with the landing page, onboarding text, notation help, and teacher message.

### Marathi

Prioritize Marathi next because Gandharva Mahavidyalaya and exam preparation communities are strong in Maharashtra. Localize the teacher handout and exam-oriented resource text.

### Bengali

Prioritize Bengali for diaspora and eastern India learners, especially where Hindustani and Rabindra Sangeet learning communities overlap. Localize the landing page and sharing messages after Hindi and Marathi.

## Analytics recommendation

Use [GoatCounter](https://www.goatcounter.com/) for privacy-friendly analytics. It supports lightweight, cookie-free analytics and suits a static GitHub Pages site.

Measure:

- Page views and unique visitors.
- Referrers by channel.
- Outbound clicks to GitHub.
- Launch post traffic spikes.
- App engagement events, if you add custom events:
  - Raga selected.
  - Tanpura started.
  - Tabla started.
  - Composition played.
  - Compose generated.
  - Recording started.
  - Saved line exported.

Do not measure:

- Microphone content.
- Saved lines.
- Recordings.
- Personal identifiers.
- Student names or teacher names.
